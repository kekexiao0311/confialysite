What's covered

What Does Car Insurance Cover in Spain?

  • Third-party liability (legal minimum). Covers injury or damage you cause to others — required by law to drive in Spain.
  • Own-vehicle damage (comprehensive). Repairs to your own car after an accident, regardless of fault, on todo riesgo plans.
  • Theft & fire. Your vehicle against theft or fire damage, usually from third-party-plus upward.
  • Glass breakage. Windscreen and window repair or replacement, often with no or low excess.
  • Driver injury cover (seguro del conductor). Injury to you specifically — third-party liability alone doesn't pay out if you're the one hurt.
  • Legal defense for traffic incidents. Legal representation after an accident or a fine dispute, standard on even basic third-party policies.
  • Roadside assistance. Towing and breakdown support, often included even on basic plans in Spain.
  • Courtesy car. A replacement vehicle while yours is in the shop, typically only on todo riesgo tiers.
  • Weather & natural events. Hail, flooding and storm damage — a separate line from collision cover, worth checking if it's included.

Frequently asked

Frequently Asked Questions About Car Insurance in Spain

Is car insurance mandatory in Spain?

Yes — at minimum, third-party liability insurance is legally required to drive, and driving without it can mean fines and your car being impounded.

How much does car insurance cost in Spain?

It varies a lot by driver age, car type, and location — your comparison shows real prices for your exact situation, not a national average that doesn't apply to you.

Will my no-claims history from another country count here?

Some insurers consider it, others don't — we show you which plans do, since it can be one of the biggest factors in your price.

Is it worth switching before my current policy renews?

Often yes — many insurers price new customers more competitively than renewals. It costs nothing to check before your renewal date locks you in.
